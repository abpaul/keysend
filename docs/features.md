## Pages and Onboarding

Signed-in pages use `assets/views/app/base.html`, `assets/static/parts.css` and `assets/static/app.css`. The public home page shows parts of the Ad Account page and of the Campaign page as examples: the same templates and builders, with the invented data of `src/monitoring/example.rs` (`src/controllers/monitoring/example.rs` builds the values), and `parts.css` for their styles. An example has no link and no form.

Onboarding has four steps: sign in with Google (`/sign-in`), connect Google Ads (`/connect`), choose up to five Ad Accounts (`/ad-accounts/choose/{id}`), and the Ad Account page (`/ad-accounts/{id}`) in its first-session state. Choosing one Ad Account lands on its page; choosing several lands on `/ad-accounts`.

The POST that saves the choice collects the last 60 days of the Account history before it redirects, so that the numbers are on the first page, and queues the first run of each chosen Ad Account with `immediate: true`. That run collects the rest of the Account history and writes the first Update at once; only the daily run waits for 04:00 in the time zone of the Ad Account. The first page loads the Update with a Turbo Frame (`/ad-accounts/{id}/first-update`, which waits up to `settings.monitoring.first_update_wait_seconds`). Turbo 8.0.23 is one static file, `assets/static/turbo.min.js`, copied from the `turbo-rails` 2.0.23 gem; it is loaded only on the first-session page, with Turbo Drive off. With scripts off, "Check for the Update" loads the page again.

Public sign-up buttons keep their bounded source through Google OAuth and save it only when the Workspace is created. Romanian home buttons have distinct sources. Each visible resource has a deterministic page key; both sign-in entry points reject unknown or future resource sources without blocking sign-in.

Every page works without scripts. Settings of an Ad Account validate every value first and save the profile, the Branding, the logo and the monitoring settings in one transaction. Beside the target cost per conversion, the Ad Account profile offers optional gross margin and share of conversions that become customers (both percentages above zero and at most 100), value of a new customer in the Ad Account currency (above zero and below one billion), and target return on ad spend (above zero and below 10,000). Empty fields clear the stored values. Invalid numbers and values outside these limits show an error beside the field and save nothing. `/settings` lists the Google Connections and deletes the Workspace.

The Ad Account page shows the latest Assessment before the Update: its Result, the 30 days it covers, what worked, what needs attention, the priorities for the next 30 days and any Market context. Each point shows its observation and, when present, a separate muted line labelled Possible explanation. Market context lists the distinct source host names of verified research facts from the latest Deep check. Each Recommendation shows its confidence (high, medium or low), its subject when one is stored, and how to check it when that text is available. Campaign names in these texts link to their pages. The Campaign tables on the Ad Account page and in its Export list every enabled Campaign, including one with zero activity, and any paused or removed Campaign with impressions, clicks or spend in the Window or the Window before.

## Account history

KeySend stores the Account history in SQLite: `campaign_days` (one row for each day, campaign, and device, for 24 months), `campaigns` (name, status, type, bid strategy, budget), and `account_changes` (each change that Google reports, with its old and new value, kept for 24 months because Google keeps change history for 30 days only). `account_history` records the collected range and the last error; `google_failures` records the last failed Google request of an Ad Account for diagnosis.

The first collection stores the last 60 completed days, then older ranges of 92 days up to 24 months, newest first. Later runs read the last 30 days again, so late conversions replace the stored days. A gap longer than 30 days restarts collection without deleting stored days.

## Google Ads client

The Google Ads REST v25 client (`src/google_ads.rs`) reads with `googleAds:search`, the stored Google Connection refresh token, and `login-customer-id` for manager access. It sends no developer token: Google retired them on 9 September 2026 and access levels belong to the Google Cloud project of the OAuth client. Currency and IANA time zone come from the Ad Account. The daily Campaign query also reads each budget's ID, whether it is shared and how many Campaigns use it (`campaign_budgets`). `src/google_ads/mutate.rs` sends Optimize changes (`campaignBudgets:mutate`, `campaigns:mutate`) only when `settings.optimize.live` is on (`docs/optimization.md`).

Basic access has a project limit of 15,000 operations per day. Set `settings.google.daily_limit` to 2,880 for Explorer access. The database reserves operations atomically across Workspaces and counts every request, including pagination.

## Monitoring

`monitoring::schedule::hourly` queues a `MonitoringJob` for each Ad Account of each Workspace at the start of every hour. It also removes the old jobs from the queue: a completed job after 7 days, a failed job after 30 days, and queues the retention job once a day (see Data retention). A run collects history, runs a Deep check if due, writes one Update per Ad Account and day, stores Alerts, and queues `EmailWorker`.

Window Metrics support exactly 7 or 30 completed days against the same number of days before. Daily trend charts and their PDF equivalents show that Window. Each day’s usual range uses up to eight earlier matching weekdays and requires at least four finite observations. The range does not depend on the displayed Window. The separate weekly charts, 30-day sparklines, Deep checks and last-eight-week Account change list keep their stated periods. A change reads whole percent from 10% upward and one decimal below 10%. Movement detection uses each Campaign's eight prior complete weeks: mean absolute weekly change plus two standard deviations, with floors of one currency unit, ten clicks, and one conversion.

When an Ad Account is not judged by conversions, the Update says nothing about results. The Ad Account page states the reason once, in the tracking notice (`view::tracking_notice`); the Weekly email states "Results cannot be judged." once, below the Metrics. The Export states the reason once in its tracking notice.

An Update states a Cause for its summary and for each Campaign line, with its level and its evidence. `src/monitoring/causes.rs` builds the candidates from the stored data after detection; AI never invents one. A candidate has a stable id, a kind, a level (Confirmed, Likely or Contribution), an evidence sentence that code writes with the formatted numbers and its anchors: the date of the Account change, "tracking", "stopped", "started", "budget" or "rank", and none for a Contribution. The kinds are checked in this order:

1. Tracking (Likely): the Conversions movement is a fall to 10% or less, or a rise to twice or more, of the previous 7 days with at least 10 conversions, with no Clicks movement and at least 100 clicks. A rise is stated as conversions counted twice.
2. Campaign stopped or started (Confirmed): the `reasons` of detection; the Campaign has no spend after it stopped.
3. Account change (Confirmed for a status change that fits a Spend or Clicks movement in the same direction, and for a budget change that fits it when the budget limited the spend: the average daily spend before a raise was at least 80% of the old daily budget, or the average daily spend after a cut is at least 80% of the new daily budget, `BUDGET_BINDING`; Likely for any other budget change, for a bid strategy or target cost per conversion change, for a change of the Ad Account itself and for any Conversions movement): a readable change in the Window, at least 2 days before the last day, after which the daily average moved in the direction of the movement and the days before it had not moved yet. Each day is compared with the same weekday one week earlier, so a weekly pattern is not read as an effect.
4. Budget limit and rank lost (Likely): the share of impressions lost to budget or to rank rose by 10 points or more to at least 20%, against the previous 7 days, for a Campaign whose Clicks or Conversions fell. It needs exact search shares (no `Share::Unknown`, no `Share::OutsideExactRange`) for at least 5 days of each Window, and 100 impressions in each.
5. Concentration (a Contribution, not a Cause): one Campaign has 60% to 100% of an Ad Account movement (at least two Campaigns served), or one device has 60% to 100% of a Campaign movement (at least two devices served). Its id holds the Campaign id.

A Campaign cause that explains most of an Ad Account movement (at least 60%) also becomes a cause of the Ad Account summary, with its name in the evidence. The Budget limit and the rank cause have no Ad Account summary of their own: the share is stored for each Campaign only. The candidates are stored in `Detection.causes`. The AI request carries them in `causes` (the summary) and in the `causes` of each Campaign. Its schema adds a `cause` field to the summary and to each line: the id of one listed candidate, or null. The check (`ai::valid_monitoring`) removes the Campaign names from the text first, so that a name cannot supply a word. It rejects causal words in a text whose cause is null or a Contribution. For a Confirmed or Likely cause the text must hold an anchor of the cause, every sentence with a causal word must hold one, and for a Likely cause that sentence must also hold "most likely" or "probably". Numbers and names still come from the facts and the evidence of the chosen candidate. The Update stores the chosen cause (level and evidence) and the page, the Weekly email and the Export show it, with the label Confirmed cause, Likely cause or Contribution. Without AI, or when the answer is rejected, `update::fallback` writes the movements first and then states the first candidate of each text in code-written words with its level ("Spend rose, most likely because of the Account change on 7 October."); a Contribution has no such sentence. A Campaign line keeps whole sentences up to 220 characters and keeps the cause only when its sentence fits.

A Follow-up (`src/monitoring/follow_up.rs`) checks how an Account change turned out. It is computed from `account_changes` and `campaign_days` whenever it is needed and is never stored as a row. It exists for a change that `google_ads::change_text` reads and that belongs to one Campaign: a daily budget that rose or fell, a Campaign status that became paused or enabled, a bid strategy, or a target cost per conversion. It compares the 14 complete days after the change with the 14 complete days before it; the day of the change belongs to neither. A Follow-up is due 16 days after the change, because conversions arrive late (`CONVERSION_MIN_AGE_DAYS`). The verdict is a rule in code: it worked, it has not worked yet, no clear effect, or cannot be judged. A difference counts only when it is outside the normal variation of the Campaign (the three periods of 14 days before the change, with the formula and floors of movement detection). A difference in conversions or clicks also has to exceed 2 times the square root of the two counts added (`COUNT_NOISE_DEVIATIONS`), and the floor of a difference in cost per result is 10% of the cost before (`MIN_COST_CHANGE_SHARE`). A raised budget worked when spend rose beyond its variation, cost per result stayed within 25% of the cost before, and results rose beyond their variation. A lowered budget worked when spend fell at least 10 points more than results. A bid strategy or target change worked when cost per result or results moved the right way and neither moved the wrong way. Cost per click and clicks replace cost per conversion and conversions for a Campaign that is not judged by conversions. When the other Campaigns of the Ad Account, taken together, moved their results in the same direction by a ratio that is at least half of the ratio of this Campaign (`SEASONAL_SIMILARITY`, and at least 10%, `SEASONAL_MIN_MOVE`, beyond the same count noise) over the same two periods, a verdict of it worked or it has not worked yet becomes no clear effect, and the sentence says that the rest of the Ad Account moved the same way; with no other Campaign that has results the control does not apply. A Follow-up cannot be judged when any other stored change of the Campaign, or any change without a Campaign, falls in the 28 days of the comparison, when the Campaign did not serve on at least 7 days of one period, or when a period has fewer than 5 conversions (100 clicks; `MIN_PERIOD_CONVERSIONS` and `MIN_PERIOD_CLICKS`, apart from the 10 conversions of Conversion tracking). A pause or a restart that no other change touches has no verdict; it states what the Campaign saved and lost, or what it has delivered since. The thresholds are named constants in the module. The daily run attaches the Follow-ups that become due that day to the Detection, so the Campaign gets a line and the Update is not quiet; the AI receives them as facts and may word them, but `update::accept` rejects a line or a summary that claims a verdict that the listed Follow-ups do not give (a text that claims anything about the verdict of Follow-ups with different verdicts, or says that a change failed, is rejected), and `update::fallback` writes the Follow-up in code, after the movements. The Campaign page shows the code-written sentence beside the Account change (through `language::Text`, in the language of the User) or, for a younger change, the day when KeySend checks the result. The Weekly email carries the Update lines, so it carries the Follow-up with them. The Campaign page follows up the changes of its 8-week list.

Deep checks collect conversion actions, Campaign budgets, search terms, keywords, landing pages, ads and locations, and compare the 30 days before the Deep check with the 30 days before them and with the same days 52 weeks earlier (`deep_check::compare`). `src/monitoring/findings.rs` detects Findings with fixed rules and each rule needs a minimum of evidence. AI never creates a Finding. Besides the rules for search terms, keywords, budgets, landing pages and ads, a Campaign whose clicks or cost per click rose by 20% without more conversions beyond chance, while its cost per conversion rose by 10%, is a Finding; so is a location with at least 25% of the spend and a cost per conversion far above the Ad Account, and, for an Ad Account judged by value (conversion value at least its spend), a Campaign whose return on ad spend is at most half of the Ad Account's. Conversions that stop for at least 5 settled days while the Ad Account still gets 100 clicks are a Conversion tracking Finding (`tracking::stopped`).

Before the Review of a new Deep check, one more OpenAI request (`business_research`) looks the business up with the web search tool: at most 3 searches, 3,000 output tokens and the 70-second limit of every AI request. It carries the name, the website of the landing page with the most clicks, the goal and note of the Ad Account profile and the names of the Campaigns and locations, and never a metric. It returns facts, each with a claim, a kind, the exact quote of its page and the page. KeySend reads each cited page itself, once, from a public address only (no private or local network, the checked address pinned, no redirect, 5 seconds, 1 MB), and marks a fact verified only when the search returned the page, the quote is on it, every number of the claim is in the quote, the business was found on its own website, and, for a fact about the business itself (what it offers, where, its hours or prices), the page is on that website (`business::accept`). The summary, the offers and the areas have no source, so they keep no digit. The research is stored with the Deep check and reused for 30 days while the website stays the same; a failed research is tried again with the next Deep check. The Assessment may write market context only from verified facts, and its numbers only from them.

One OpenAI request (`monitoring_review`, `settings.services.ai_model`) writes the Assessment and proposes Recommendations. Code sets the Result (`assessment::judge`): a change in conversions counts only beyond 2 times the square root of the two counts added, a cost per conversion or a return on ad spend only from 10%, and a fall that the same days of last year also had is seasonal. The request carries the formatted evidence (each metric, its change, the share of each Campaign and location, the targets), the Business context with the lens of its kind (`business::Kind`), and the Findings; it is skipped when the Result is unclear and no Finding can get a Recommendation. KeySend keeps a Recommendation only when it gives the key of a detected Finding, names a subject of that Finding (a landing page by its path, an ad by its first headline), uses numbers from that Finding or the evidence, asks for more spend only for a budget-limited Finding, and does not reverse a recorded budget change (`src/monitoring/recommendation.rs`); its measure says how to check it. Recommendation identity is the rule and Campaign ID; closed Recommendations stay closed for 90 days. `src/monitoring/eval.rs` and `scripts/ai-eval/` evaluate the written analysis on synthetic Ad Accounts against what a consultant concludes from them.

The Ad Account profile may give a margin, the value of a customer, the share of conversions that become customers and a target return on ad spend (`economics::Economics`). From them code computes the break-even return (1 divided by the margin) and the break-even cost per conversion (customer value times the close rate, times the margin when one is given), and a Campaign below the break-even return or above the break-even cost is a Finding that replaces the relative cost and return rules. Conversion tracking health has two more rules (`tracking::spike`, `tracking::lag`): conversions per click that double over the last 7 settled days while clicks per day hold are a Conversion tracking Finding, and when the conversion lag buckets of Google Ads show that at least 3% of the conversions of the last 30 days have probably not arrived yet, the Result compares the counts with the missing conversions added and says so. Each point of what worked or needs attention is an observation that cites its evidence (the result, the Ad Account, a Campaign, a location, a Finding, an earlier Recommendation or the business) and may add an explanation; the numbers of both must come from the cited evidence, a percent may not move against it, and a reason may be named only in a sentence that says it is possible (`claims::point`, `ai::valid_hedged`). A Recommendation stores its intent, the subject that its action names and a confidence: that of its rule, one level lower when the Ad Account has fewer than 30 conversions or misses late conversions (`recommendation::confidence`); its measure must name a value to compare with. The Review lists the earlier Recommendations of the last 180 days with what followed each one after 14 settled days, the Campaign's results since against the 30 days before, in a sentence that names no effect (`earlier::Earlier`), and refuses a Recommendation that repeats the intent and subject of one that is open or closed less than 90 days ago.

Stored text (Updates, Findings, Recommendations, Account changes) stays English in the database. For a user whose language is not English, the queue worker translates it with the AI after a run and after the user changes the language (`TranslationWorker`). The AI sees each text with its names, numbers, amounts, percents and dates replaced by placeholders (`src/translation/mask.rs`); KeySend writes them back with the formats of the language and stores only an answer that keeps every placeholder and adds no digit or markup. The `translations` table keys a translation by Workspace, language and the hash of the English text. Pages and emails read it in one statement and show the English original, marked `lang="en"`, until a translation exists. A Romanian Export waits until its required stored text has a translation. English users read the stored text and no translation runs. The navbar saves the User language across requests. Exports use Romanian for a Romanian User and English for every other language; there is no separate Export language control.

`WorkspaceStore::monitoring_access` checks a fourteen-day Trial or an active Subscription. The first sign-in creates the Workspace and starts its Trial. Up to five Ad Accounts are allowed, from `access::AD_ACCOUNT_LIMIT`; no Workspace stores its own allowance. Public content resolves `{ad_account_limit}` from the same constant when its catalog loads. The Subscription costs 4,900 USD cents a month.

The Export (`/ad-accounts/{id}/export.pdf`) is a PDF written with the Typst library. Liberation Sans Regular and Bold are embedded in the application. The font license is in `assets/static/fonts/Liberation-LICENSE`. Two renders can run at once. The Export draws a logo only when the Ad Account has one.

`/sample-report.pdf` opens a committed English Export of the fictional Harbour Dental Ad Account without signing in; every page labels the sample data.

The Export keeps the separate Assessment explanations, verified Market context source hosts, and Recommendation confidence and subjects of the page. Observations and explanations use their own stored translations. The Export of an Ad Account prints what its page and its breakdowns page show for the Window, in this order: any required data-quality explanation, the Assessment with its Result and period when available, the Update with each Campaign line and its Cause, the Metrics with a sparkline each, the Recommendations with their Finding and how to check them, the Campaigns with every Metric and its value in the days before, the charts (a trend for each Metric that the page offers, with the Account changes of the 8 weeks and their Follow-ups below the spend trend), the charts of the Deep check, and the earlier Updates of the Window. The Export of a Campaign prints what the Campaign page shows: its Updates, Metrics, Recommendations and other Findings, its charts with the Account changes and their Follow-ups, and its Deep check. `controllers::export::ad_account_export` and `campaign_export` build the Export from the view builders of the pages; `monitoring::export::source` writes it in Typst.

Settings keys: `services.ai_url`, `services.ai_model`, optional `services.ai_prices`, `services.email_url`, and `services.test_now` (tests only).

## Payments

Stripe provides hosted Checkout and the Subscription portal. The prefix of `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` selects the Stripe mode: `sk_test_` or `rk_test_` for test mode, `sk_live_` or `rk_live_` for live mode. KeySend refuses to start with another key, and accepts only Stripe objects and webhook events of the selected mode. A 49 USD monthly Subscription keeps KeySend open for up to five Ad Accounts while it is active and its paid period has not ended.

Checkout charges the Stripe price with the lookup key `keysend_monthly`, and refuses when Stripe has no such price or the price is not 4,900 USD cents a month. Checkout uses Stripe Managed Payments: Stripe sells the Subscription through Link as the merchant of record, calculates the tax from the billing address, adds it to the price, and remits it. Stripe sends the receipts, the invoices and the failed-payment emails from Link, and the User can also manage the Subscription on link.com. The Subscription portal shows the invoices and lets the User change the card, the billing details and the tax ID, and cancel at the end of the paid period.

When a payment fails (`past_due` or `unpaid`), Access stops, `/subscription` sends the User to the portal to change the card, and no second Checkout starts.

Only a verified Stripe webhook can grant access. Checkout redirects grant nothing. Each webhook reads the current Stripe object before storing its result, and repeated event IDs have no further effect. A paid Checkout counts only when its amount before tax is the Subscription price. A late Checkout for an older Subscription cannot replace a newer one.

The task `stripe_setup` prepares a Stripe account, and a second run creates nothing more: `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_… cargo loco task stripe_setup`. A restricted key needs write access to Tax settings, Products, Prices, Customer portal and Webhook endpoints. It sets the Stripe Tax defaults (the tax code for business software as a service, tax added to the price), creates the product `keysend_subscription` and the price `keysend_monthly`, and configures the Subscription portal. On an HTTPS host it also creates the webhook at `/stripe/webhook` with API version `2025-03-31.basil`, for `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, and `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`, and `customer.subscription.deleted`. It stops when Stripe Tax has no head office address. The product tax code must be one that Managed Payments accepts; `txcd_10103001` is one.

Payment routes are `/subscription`, `/subscription/portal`, `/billing` (a redirect), and `/stripe/webhook`. The Workspace-scoped tables are `stripe_customers`, `billing_checkouts`, `subscriptions`, and `stripe_events`.

## Account controls

`/settings` links to confirmation pages for removing a Google Connection and for deleting the User and Workspace; each POST verifies ownership and requires `confirm=remove`. An Ad Account is removed at `/ad-accounts/{id}/remove`. Google Connection removal revokes its Google refresh token. User deletion revokes every Google refresh token, deletes its scoped Stripe customer, which cancels active subscriptions, records the Trial hash, and deletes the rows (`removal::delete_workspace`; the retention job uses the same function). Failed external calls retain local data for retry. Removing an Ad Account does not revoke the Google Connection.

## Data retention

`monitoring::schedule::hourly` queues a `RetentionJob` in the 03:00 UTC hour. `RetentionWorker` (`src/monitoring/retention.rs`) applies five rules with `Settings::now()` (month limits use calendar months, `chrono::Months`):

1. Inactive Workspace: when the Access of a Workspace ended before 12 months ago, the job deletes the Workspace with `removal::delete_workspace`, the function behind User deletion in `/settings`. The Access ended at the later of the end of the Trial and the end of the paid period of the Subscription (`access::access_ended_at`). A Workspace whose User has a session that has not expired stays (`identity::signed_in`), so a returning User whose remembered Trial ended long ago keeps the new Workspace while signed in. When Google or Stripe does not confirm, no row is deleted and the next run tries again.
2. Trial hash: the job deletes the `trial_history` rows whose `started_at` is before 24 months ago. A User who signs up again after that gets a new Trial.
3. Account history: for each Ad Account, the job deletes the rows whose day is before the date 24 months before today in the time zone of the Ad Account (the same date that limits the first collection). The tables are `campaign_days`, `campaign_network_days`, `campaign_search_share_days`, `conversion_action_days`, `conversion_lag_totals`, `demographic_totals`, `hour_totals`, `keyword_quality`, `location_totals`, `updates`, `alerts`, `alert_evaluations`, `monitoring_runs`, `account_changes` (by `changed_at`), `monitoring_recommendations` (state `done` or `not_useful`, by `closed_day`) and `monitoring_findings` (inactive, by `last_day`; their Recommendations go with them). An active Finding and an open Recommendation stay. `weekly_emails`, `deep_checks`, `campaigns` and the settings of an Ad Account stay.

4. Measurement: delete Workspace activity, anonymous operation totals and business snapshots before the UTC date 24 months ago. Subscription changes stay with their Workspace until removal.

5. Agent investigations: delete a whole thread with its steps only when its newest investigation is more than 180 days old. Removing an Ad Account deletes investigations whose stored Ad Account list contains it and their follow-ups; removing a Workspace deletes all its investigations and steps.

## Product measurement

`src/workspace/measurement.rs` records daily page views, delivered Exports and feedback separately
from Connected, Synced and Assessed activation activity. This covers Ad Account pages, Campaign
pages and breakdowns, choosing Ad Accounts, feedback, the daily run and the Export. Global daily
operation totals cover collection, Deep checks, Export rendering, Google Ads requests and AI
requests (requested model, nullable usage tokens, duration and outcome). These totals hold no
Workspace identifiers. The hourly scheduler keeps one business snapshot per UTC day, using the
current local Trial and Subscription states. Subscription changes preserve observed status and
scheduled-cancellation transitions without changing Access. Tests are in
`tests/requests/app/monitoring/measurement.rs` and `tests/requests/app/billing.rs`.
The Data retention job purges the three daily measurement tables after 24 months; removing a
Workspace also removes its activity and Subscription changes. The privacy page explains usage
measurement. No prices are configured by default; AI token costs need explicit model prices and
are estimates.

Optimize also supports an explicitly consented Shadow policy. The same decision pipeline stores immutable evaluations in `src/optimize/shadow.rs` and `src/workspace/shadow.rs`, separate from executable Proposals. `/optimize/shadows/{id}` shows proposed changes, abstentions, feedback and descriptive observations. Shadow has no approval or execution path; the authorization and REST adapter also refuse changes while an account is in Shadow. The migration is `m20261021_000022_shadow`, with request coverage in `tests/requests/app/shadow.rs` and `tests/requests/migration.rs`. The commercial case fixture is `tests/fixtures/optimize-commercial-independent.json`. Its expected decisions are independent provisional judgments, not evidence of commercial uplift.

Optimize hardening also includes provider-confirmation recovery at `POST /optimize/proposals/{id}/recover`: an unverified User report closes only an Uncertain execution, leaves Investigate/Suspended, retires pending approvals and never sends to Google Ads. Request coverage is in `tests/requests/app/uncertain_recovery.rs`. `tests/requests/app/shadow_retention.rs` covers observation completion, exact own-change provenance, changed-resource collection and account-scoped snapshot retention.
