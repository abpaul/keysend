## Pages and Onboarding

Signed-in pages use `assets/views/app/base.html`, `assets/static/parts.css` and `assets/static/app.css`. The public home page shows parts of the Ad Account page and of the Campaign page as examples: the same templates and builders, with the invented data of `src/monitoring/example.rs` (`src/controllers/monitoring/example.rs` builds the values), and `parts.css` for their styles. An example has no link and no form.

Onboarding has four steps: sign in with Google (`/sign-in`), connect Google Ads (`/connect`), choose up to three Ad Accounts (`/ad-accounts/choose/{id}`), and the Ad Account page (`/ad-accounts/{id}`) in its first-session state. Choosing one Ad Account lands on its page; choosing several lands on `/ad-accounts`.

The POST that saves the choice collects the last 60 days of the Account history before it redirects, so that the numbers are on the first page, and queues the first run of each chosen Ad Account with `immediate: true`. That run collects the rest of the Account history and writes the first Update at once; only the daily run waits for 04:00 in the time zone of the Ad Account. The first page loads the Update with a Turbo Frame (`/ad-accounts/{id}/first-update`, which waits up to `settings.monitoring.first_update_wait_seconds`). Turbo 8.0.23 is one static file, `assets/static/turbo.min.js`, copied from the `turbo-rails` 2.0.23 gem; it is loaded only on the first-session page, with Turbo Drive off. With scripts off, "Check for the Update" loads the page again.

Every page works without scripts. Settings of an Ad Account validate every value first and save the profile, the Branding, the logo and the monitoring settings in one transaction. `/settings` lists the Google Connections and deletes the Workspace.

## Account history

KeySend stores the Account history in SQLite: `campaign_days` (one row for each day, campaign, and device, for 24 months), `campaigns` (name, status, type, bid strategy, budget), and `account_changes` (each change that Google reports, with its old and new value, kept permanently because Google keeps change history for 30 days only). `account_history` records the collected range and the last error; `google_failures` records the last failed Google request of an Ad Account for diagnosis.

The first collection stores the last 60 completed days, then older ranges of 92 days up to 24 months, newest first. Later runs read the last 30 days again, so late conversions replace the stored days. A gap longer than 30 days restarts collection without deleting stored days.

## Google Ads client

The Google Ads REST v25 client (`src/google_ads.rs`) uses only `googleAds:search`, the stored Google Connection refresh token, and `login-customer-id` for manager access. It sends no developer token. Currency and IANA time zone come from the Ad Account.

Basic access has a project limit of 15,000 operations per day. Set `settings.google.daily_limit` to 2,880 for Explorer access. The database reserves operations atomically across Workspaces and counts every request, including pagination.

## Monitoring

`monitoring::schedule::hourly` queues a `MonitoringJob` for each Ad Account of each Workspace at the start of every hour. It also removes the old jobs from the queue: a completed job after 7 days, a failed job after 30 days. A run collects history, runs a Deep check if due, writes one Update per Ad Account and day, stores Alerts, and queues `EmailWorker`.

Window Metrics support exactly 7 or 30 completed days against the same number of days before. A change reads whole percent from 10% upward and one decimal below 10%. Movement detection uses each Campaign's eight prior complete weeks: mean absolute weekly change plus two standard deviations, with floors of one currency unit, ten clicks, and one conversion.

When an Ad Account is not judged by conversions, the Update says nothing about results. The Ad Account page states the reason once, in the tracking notice (`view::tracking_notice`); the Weekly email and the Export state "Results cannot be judged." once, below the Metrics.

An Update states a Cause for its summary and for each Campaign line, with its level and its evidence. `src/monitoring/causes.rs` builds the candidates from the stored data after detection; AI never invents one. A candidate has a stable id, a kind, a level (Confirmed, Likely or Contribution), an evidence sentence that code writes with the formatted numbers and its anchors: the date of the Account change, "tracking", "stopped", "started", "budget" or "rank", and none for a Contribution. The kinds are checked in this order:

1. Tracking (Likely): the Conversions movement is a fall to 10% or less of a previous 7 days with at least 10 conversions, with no Clicks movement and at least 100 clicks.
2. Campaign stopped or started (Confirmed): the `reasons` of detection; the Campaign has no spend after it stopped.
3. Account change (Confirmed for a status change that fits a Spend or Clicks movement in the same direction, and for a budget change that fits it when the budget limited the spend: the average daily spend before a raise was at least 80% of the old daily budget, or the average daily spend after a cut is at least 80% of the new daily budget, `BUDGET_BINDING`; Likely for any other budget change, for a bid strategy or target cost per conversion change, for a change of the Ad Account itself and for any Conversions movement): a readable change in the Window, at least 2 days before the last day, after which the daily average moved in the direction of the movement and the days before it had not moved yet. Each day is compared with the same weekday one week earlier, so a weekly pattern is not read as an effect.
4. Budget limit and rank lost (Likely): the share of impressions lost to budget or to rank rose by 10 points or more to at least 20%, against the previous 7 days, for a Campaign whose Clicks or Conversions fell. It needs exact search shares (no `Share::Unknown`, no `Share::OutsideExactRange`) for at least 5 days of each Window, and 100 impressions in each.
5. Concentration (a Contribution, not a Cause): one Campaign has 60% to 100% of an Ad Account movement (at least two Campaigns served), or one device has 60% to 100% of a Campaign movement (at least two devices served). Its id holds the Campaign id.

A Campaign cause that explains most of an Ad Account movement (at least 60%) also becomes a cause of the Ad Account summary, with its name in the evidence. The Budget limit and the rank cause have no Ad Account summary of their own: the share is stored for each Campaign only. The candidates are stored in `Detection.causes`. The AI request carries them in `causes` (the summary) and in the `causes` of each Campaign. Its schema adds a `cause` field to the summary and to each line: the id of one listed candidate, or null. The check (`ai::valid_monitoring`) removes the Campaign names from the text first, so that a name cannot supply a word. It rejects causal words in a text whose cause is null or a Contribution. For a Confirmed or Likely cause the text must hold an anchor of the cause, every sentence with a causal word must hold one, and for a Likely cause that sentence must also hold "most likely" or "probably". Numbers and names still come from the facts and the evidence of the chosen candidate. The Update stores the chosen cause (level and evidence) and the page, the Weekly email and the Export show it, with the label Confirmed cause, Likely cause or Contribution. Without AI, or when the answer is rejected, `update::fallback` writes the movements first and then states the first candidate of each text in code-written words with its level ("Spend rose, most likely because of the Account change on 7 October."); a Contribution has no such sentence. A Campaign line keeps whole sentences up to 220 characters and keeps the cause only when its sentence fits.

A Follow-up (`src/monitoring/follow_up.rs`) checks how an Account change turned out. It is computed from `account_changes` and `campaign_days` whenever it is needed and is never stored as a row. It exists for a change that `google_ads::change_text` reads and that belongs to one Campaign: a daily budget that rose or fell, a Campaign status that became paused or enabled, a bid strategy, or a target cost per conversion. It compares the 14 complete days after the change with the 14 complete days before it; the day of the change belongs to neither. A Follow-up is due 16 days after the change, because conversions arrive late (`CONVERSION_MIN_AGE_DAYS`). The verdict is a rule in code: it worked, it has not worked yet, no clear effect, or cannot be judged. A difference counts only when it is outside the normal variation of the Campaign (the three periods of 14 days before the change, with the formula and floors of movement detection). A difference in conversions or clicks also has to exceed 2 times the square root of the two counts added (`COUNT_NOISE_DEVIATIONS`), and the floor of a difference in cost per result is 10% of the cost before (`MIN_COST_CHANGE_SHARE`). A raised budget worked when spend rose beyond its variation, cost per result stayed within 25% of the cost before, and results rose beyond their variation. A lowered budget worked when spend fell at least 10 points more than results. A bid strategy or target change worked when cost per result or results moved the right way and neither moved the wrong way. Cost per click and clicks replace cost per conversion and conversions for a Campaign that is not judged by conversions. When the other Campaigns of the Ad Account, taken together, moved their results in the same direction by a ratio that is at least half of the ratio of this Campaign (`SEASONAL_SIMILARITY`, and at least 10%, `SEASONAL_MIN_MOVE`, beyond the same count noise) over the same two periods, a verdict of it worked or it has not worked yet becomes no clear effect, and the sentence says that the rest of the Ad Account moved the same way; with no other Campaign that has results the control does not apply. A Follow-up cannot be judged when any other stored change of the Campaign, or any change without a Campaign, falls in the 28 days of the comparison, when the Campaign did not serve on at least 7 days of one period, or when a period has fewer than 5 conversions (100 clicks; `MIN_PERIOD_CONVERSIONS` and `MIN_PERIOD_CLICKS`, apart from the 10 conversions of Conversion tracking). A pause or a restart that no other change touches has no verdict; it states what the Campaign saved and lost, or what it has delivered since. The thresholds are named constants in the module. The daily run attaches the Follow-ups that become due that day to the Detection, so the Campaign gets a line and the Update is not quiet; the AI receives them as facts and may word them, but `update::accept` rejects a line or a summary that claims a verdict that the listed Follow-ups do not give (a text that claims anything about the verdict of Follow-ups with different verdicts, or says that a change failed, is rejected), and `update::fallback` writes the Follow-up in code, after the movements. The Campaign page shows the code-written sentence beside the Account change (through `language::Text`, in the language of the User) or, for a younger change, the day when KeySend checks the result. The Weekly email carries the Update lines, so it carries the Follow-up with them. The Campaign page follows up the changes of its 8-week list.

Deep checks collect conversion actions, Campaign budgets, search terms, keywords, landing pages, and ads. `src/monitoring/findings.rs` detects Findings with fixed rules and each rule needs a minimum of evidence. AI never creates a Finding. One OpenAI request (`settings.services.ai_model`) proposes Recommendations; KeySend accepts one only when it gives the key of a detected Finding, names a subject of that Finding, uses numbers from that Finding or the Ad Account, asks for more spend only for a budget-limit Finding, and does not reverse a recorded budget change (`src/monitoring/recommendation.rs`). Recommendation identity is the rule and Campaign ID; closed Recommendations stay closed for 90 days.

Stored text (Updates, Findings, Recommendations, Account changes) stays English in the database. For a user whose language is not English, the queue worker translates it with the AI after a run and after the user changes the language (`TranslationWorker`). The AI sees each text with its names, numbers, amounts, percents and dates replaced by placeholders (`src/translation/mask.rs`); KeySend writes them back with the formats of the language and stores only an answer that keeps every placeholder and adds no digit or markup. The `translations` table keys a translation by Workspace, language and the hash of the English text. Pages, Exports and emails read it in one statement and show the English original, marked `lang="en"`, until a translation exists. English users read the stored text and no translation runs.

`WorkspaceStore::monitoring_access` checks a fourteen-day Trial or an active Subscription. The first sign-in creates the Workspace and starts its Trial. Up to three Ad Accounts are allowed. The Subscription costs 4,900 USD cents a month.

The Export (`/ad-accounts/{id}/export.pdf`) is a PDF written with the Typst library. Liberation Sans Regular and Bold are embedded in the application. The font license is in `assets/static/fonts/Liberation-LICENSE`. Two renders can run at once. The Export draws a logo only when the Ad Account has one.

Settings keys: `services.ai_url`, `services.ai_model`, `services.email_url`, and `services.test_now` (tests only).

## Payments

Stripe provides hosted Checkout and the Subscription portal. The prefix of `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` selects the Stripe mode: `sk_test_` or `rk_test_` for test mode, `sk_live_` or `rk_live_` for live mode. KeySend refuses to start with another key, and accepts only Stripe objects and webhook events of the selected mode. A 49 USD monthly Subscription keeps KeySend open for up to three Ad Accounts while it is active and its paid period has not ended.

Checkout charges the Stripe price with the lookup key `keysend_monthly`, and refuses when Stripe has no such price or the price is not 4,900 USD cents a month. Checkout uses Stripe Managed Payments: Stripe sells the Subscription through Link as the merchant of record, calculates the tax from the billing address, adds it to the price, and remits it. Stripe sends the receipts, the invoices and the failed-payment emails from Link, and the User can also manage the Subscription on link.com. The Subscription portal shows the invoices and lets the User change the card, the billing details and the tax ID, and cancel at the end of the paid period.

When a payment fails (`past_due` or `unpaid`), Access stops, `/subscription` sends the User to the portal to change the card, and no second Checkout starts.

Only a verified Stripe webhook can grant access. Checkout redirects grant nothing. Each webhook reads the current Stripe object before storing its result, and repeated event IDs have no further effect. A paid Checkout counts only when its amount before tax is the Subscription price. A late Checkout for an older Subscription cannot replace a newer one.

The task `stripe_setup` prepares a Stripe account, and a second run creates nothing more: `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY=sk_test_… cargo loco task stripe_setup`. A restricted key needs write access to Tax settings, Products, Prices, Customer portal and Webhook endpoints. It sets the Stripe Tax defaults (the tax code for business software as a service, tax added to the price), creates the product `keysend_subscription` and the price `keysend_monthly`, and configures the Subscription portal. On an HTTPS host it also creates the webhook at `/stripe/webhook` with API version `2025-03-31.basil`, for `checkout.session.completed`, `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`, and `customer.subscription.created`, `customer.subscription.updated`, and `customer.subscription.deleted`. It stops when Stripe Tax has no head office address. The product tax code must be one that Managed Payments accepts; `txcd_10103001` is one.

Payment routes are `/subscription`, `/subscription/portal`, `/billing` (a redirect), and `/stripe/webhook`. The Workspace-scoped tables are `stripe_customers`, `billing_checkouts`, `subscriptions`, and `stripe_events`.

## Account controls

`/settings` links to confirmation pages for removing a Google Connection and for deleting the User and Workspace; each POST verifies ownership and requires `confirm=remove`. An Ad Account is removed at `/ad-accounts/{id}/remove`. Google Connection removal revokes its Google refresh token. User deletion also deletes its scoped Stripe customer, which cancels active subscriptions. Failed external calls retain local data for retry. Removing an Ad Account does not revoke the Google Connection.
